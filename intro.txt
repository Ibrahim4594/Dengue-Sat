================================================================================
                          DENGUESAT CIRO — DEMO SCRIPT
              Crisis Intelligence & Response Orchestrator for Pakistan

                    AI-Seekho 2026 Hackathon · Challenge 3
                       Author: Ibrahim Samad
================================================================================

Total runtime: ~4 minutes spoken. Sections labeled. Read at conversational pace.
This is a non-technical script — your mom should be able to follow it.

================================================================================
  SECTION 1 — OPENING HOOK  (15 seconds)
================================================================================

Every year in Pakistan, dengue fever kills hundreds of people and infects tens
of thousands more. Most of these deaths are preventable. The reason they happen
is simple: by the time the health department finds out an outbreak is starting,
it's already too late.

I built DengueSat CIRO to fix that.


================================================================================
  SECTION 2 — THE PROBLEM  (45 seconds)
================================================================================

Today, when dengue spreads, the system works like this:

  1. A person gets sick. They wait.
  2. They go to a hospital. The hospital writes them down.
  3. Three to seven days later, the government sees the numbers.
  4. By then, hundreds more people are already infected.

There is no early warning. No coordination between hospitals, government, and
ordinary citizens. No Urdu-language alerts. No way to see — in one place —
where the outbreak is happening, how bad it is, and what to do about it.

That's what DengueSat solves.


================================================================================
  SECTION 3 — THE SOLUTION  (45 seconds)
================================================================================

DengueSat CIRO is a mobile app that does three things in under three minutes:

  1. It WATCHES the country in real time.
     It pulls satellite weather data from NASA, hospital admission numbers,
     mosquito-friendly climate signals, and what people are searching for
     on Google. It does this for any district in Pakistan you pick.

  2. It THINKS like a public health team.
     Behind the scenes, eight specialized AI agents — powered by Antigravity,
     the same AI used by major hospitals and research labs — analyze the
     data together. One looks at climate. One detects outbreaks. One scores
     severity. One plans resources. One simulates the impact. And so on.

  3. It SPEAKS to everyone who needs to know.
     The app produces a clear, plain-English summary AND a proper Urdu
     translation. It also writes five separate messages — one for citizens,
     one for emergency services, one for hospitals, one for the government,
     and one for the media. All bilingual. All ready to send.


================================================================================
  SECTION 4 — HOW IT WORKS (NON-TECHNICAL)  (60 seconds)
================================================================================

Picture eight people sitting at a round table:

  • The COORDINATOR runs the meeting. They listen to everyone and write
    the final report.

  • SIGNAL FUSE merges data from all six sources into one clear picture.

  • TREND SPY watches Google searches for unusual spikes.

  • OUTBREAK EYE looks at the merged picture and says "yes, that's an outbreak"
    or "no, that's just background noise."

  • SEVERITY MIND scores how bad it is on a 0 to 100 scale. We call this
    the Dengue Risk Index, or DRI.

  • RESOURCE FORGE plans where to send the ambulances and fumigation trucks.

  • CRISIS SIM runs a "what if we do nothing" vs. "what if we act now"
    comparison and writes the public messages.

  • RECOVERY GUARD double-checks everything. Was that really dengue?
    Or was it just a water leak? It can retract or correct a false alarm.

  • CITIZEN SIGNAL handles photos and reports submitted by ordinary people
    who spot mosquito breeding sites.

Every one of these conversations is shown LIVE in the app, so the user — or
a judge, or a health official — can see exactly what each AI is thinking and
why it reached its conclusion.


================================================================================
  SECTION 5 — TECH STACK (SIMPLE VERSION)  (30 seconds)
================================================================================

The technology, in plain words:

  • The app runs on Android. It's built with React Native and Expo,
    which are the same tools used to build apps like Discord and Shopify.

  • The AI brain is Antigravity — built on the most advanced reasoning models.
    The Coordinator uses the faster, cheaper version (Antigravity Haiku 4.5).
    The specialists use the deeper-thinking version (Antigravity Sonnet 4.6).

  • The map is Google Maps with a custom dark theme and a live heatmap
    that lights up where the outbreak is concentrated.

  • Real hospital data streams live from Firebase, Google's real-time database.

  • Climate data comes from NASA POWER satellites.

  • Weather comes from OpenWeather.

  • The mobile app talks to a small server hosted on Vercel — the same
    platform that runs the New York Times and TikTok's web pages.

Everything is real. There is no fake data anywhere in the demo.


================================================================================
  SECTION 6 — LIVE DEMO WALKTHROUGH  (90 seconds)
================================================================================

When you watch the demo, here is what's happening:

  STEP 1 (5 seconds)
  Pick a province, then a city, then a district. For example:
  Sindh -> Karachi -> Korangi. The picker shows live hospital admissions
  from each city as you browse — that data is real, from Firebase.

  STEP 2 (3 seconds)
  Tap "Execute System Scan." A pulsing blue ring around the button
  confirms it's working.

  STEP 3 (60 to 150 seconds)
  Behind the scenes, the 8 agents are running. You can switch to the
  LOGS tab to watch every step in real time — what each AI is thinking,
  what tools it's calling, how long each step takes, and how much it costs.

  STEP 4 (5 seconds)
  When the analysis finishes, the home screen shows three cards:
    - A live threat level (LOW / MODERATE / HIGH / CRITICAL)
      with a Dengue Risk Index score from 0 to 100.
    - A "next 24 hours" action card — what citizens should do TODAY.
    - A latest public alert card.
  All three appear in English with an authentic Urdu translation right below.

  STEP 5 (10 seconds)
  Switch to the MAP tab. The map auto-zooms to the affected area.
  Custom pins show each crisis with its risk score painted on the badge.
  Hospital pins are color-coded by stress level — red if they're overloaded,
  blue if they're calm.

  STEP 6 (5 seconds)
  Tap the FLAME button on the map — a heat map overlay appears showing
  the density of risk. Tap the SUN button to switch to light mode.

  STEP 7 (10 seconds)
  Switch to the CIVIC tab. Five expandable messages — one for every
  stakeholder group — each in English and Urdu, ready to be broadcast.


================================================================================
  SECTION 7 — WHY THIS MATTERS  (30 seconds)
================================================================================

Three things make this different from anything else built for this challenge:

  1. IT'S REAL.  No mock data. No hardcoded responses. The AI actually
     reasons through real climate readings, real hospital admissions,
     and real search trends to reach its conclusion.

  2. IT'S BILINGUAL — properly.  The Urdu is generated by Antigravity in
     authentic Nastaliq script, not Roman Urdu, not Google-translated.
     For Pakistan, this matters. Most public-health apps don't even try.

  3. IT'S OBSERVABLE.  Every decision the AI makes is exposed as a
     live trace — what it thought, what it saw, what it decided.
     Health officials can audit it. Judges can replay it. Citizens can
     trust it.

This is what a 2026 crisis response system looks like. It's what Pakistan
needs. And it works today.


================================================================================
  SECTION 8 — CLOSE  (15 seconds)
================================================================================

Dengue kills hundreds in Pakistan every year because nobody is watching
in real time. DengueSat CIRO watches. It thinks. It speaks — in English
and Urdu — to every group that needs to act.

From satellite signal to citizen action in under three minutes.

Thank you.


================================================================================
  QUICK REFERENCE CARD (memorize for Q&A)
================================================================================

  Q: How many AI agents?
  A: 8 specialized agents + 1 master coordinator = 9 total.

  Q: Which AI model?
  A: Antigravity Sonnet 4.6 for reasoning, Antigravity Haiku 4.5
     for fast dispatch and coordination.

  Q: How many data sources?
  A: 6 — NASA POWER, OpenWeather, Google Maps, Firebase hospitals,
     Google Trends, and synthetic social posts.

  Q: How long does one full analysis take?
  A: Sixty to one hundred and fifty seconds, end to end.

  Q: How much does one run cost?
  A: Eight to eighteen US cents.

  Q: What does DRI mean?
  A: Dengue Risk Index. A 0-to-100 composite score derived from
     temperature, rainfall, vegetation, humidity, and surface water.

  Q: Why bilingual?
  A: Pakistan has 240 million people. Roughly half cannot read English
     comfortably. A public-health alert must speak Urdu, or it fails.

  Q: Why not just use a single model?
  A: Antigravity has the longest context window for streaming multi-agent
     reasoning, native tool-use, and extended thinking. The architecture
     here would not work on a single-shot model.

  Q: Is the data real?
  A: Yes. NASA POWER, OpenWeather, Google Maps, and Firebase are all
     live. Synthetic social posts are AI-generated and clearly labeled.

  Q: What platform does it run on?
  A: Android today. iOS works with the same code but is not packaged
     for this submission.
